Google's Agentic Search Revolution: How Gemini 3.5 Flash Is Redefining the Internet

📅 Last Updated: May 27, 2026?? Written by Hussein Harby?? 10 min read

At Google I/O 2026, Sundar Pichai unveiled what may be the most significant change to Google Search in its 28-year history. Powered by the new Gemini 3.5 Flash model, Google is transforming from a search engine into an agentic AI platform — one that doesn't just find information, but actively works on your behalf to complete tasks, monitor topics, and generate custom interfaces on the fly.

This isn't an incremental update. It's a fundamental reimagining of what "searching the internet" means. And it has massive implications for users, businesses, and the entire web ecosystem.

What is "Agentic Search"?

Traditional search works like a library: you ask a question, and the engine points you to relevant books (websites). Agentic search works like a personal research assistant: you describe what you need, and the AI goes out, gathers information from multiple sources, synthesizes it, and presents a custom answer — sometimes even taking actions on your behalf.

The key differences:

FeatureTraditional SearchAgentic Search
InputKeywords and phrasesMultimodal: text, images, files, video
OutputList of blue linksCustom UI, interactive elements, synthesized answers
InteractionSingle query ? resultsMulti-step conversations and task execution
Follow-upNew search requiredBackground agents monitor topics 24/7
PersonalizationBasic (location, history)Deep context awareness across sessions
?? Think of It This Way: Traditional Google is like asking a librarian "Where are the books about Italian cooking?" Agentic Google is like hiring a personal chef who researches recipes, compares ingredients, checks prices at local stores, and presents you with a meal plan — all from a single request.

Gemini 3.5 Flash: The Engine Under the Hood

Powering this transformation is Gemini 3.5 Flash, released May 19, 2026. It's the first model in Google's new 3.5 generation, and it's specifically designed for speed and agency:

  • 4x faster than comparable frontier models in output tokens per second — critical for real-time search interactions.
  • "Agentic by design": Optimized for long-horizon tasks, autonomous workflows, and iterative problem-solving.
  • Outperforms Gemini 3.1 Pro on key benchmarks including Terminal-Bench 2.1 and GDPval-AA.
  • Available everywhere: Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, Google AI Studio, Gemini API, and the new Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform.

The Four Pillars of Google's New Search

1. The Intelligent Search Box

Google's search box has been reimagined as a dynamic, multimodal interface. Instead of just typing text, you can drag and drop images, paste files, share video clips, and combine multiple inputs in a single query. The AI then understands the combined intent and produces a comprehensive response.

2. Generative UI

This is perhaps the most visually dramatic change. Instead of showing the same template of blue links for every query, Search now generates custom layouts on the fly:

  • Search for a recipe ? get an interactive ingredient list with serving-size adjusters.
  • Compare two products ? get a side-by-side comparison table generated in real time.
  • Research a scientific concept ? get an interactive simulation or visualization.

3. Information Agents

Perhaps the most revolutionary feature: personalized AI agents that run in the background 24/7, monitoring topics you care about and providing synthesized updates. Examples:

  • Set an agent to monitor housing prices in your target neighborhood — it alerts you when something matches your criteria.
  • Track a specific technology topic — the agent reads and summarizes relevant articles daily.
  • Monitor competitor pricing — the agent flags changes and trends.

Information agents are currently rolling out to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.

4. Gemini Spark

A personal AI agent that works across Google's ecosystem — managing workflows in Gmail, Calendar, Drive, and Docs. Think of it as a digital executive assistant that actually understands context across all your Google tools.

The Controversy: Who Benefits?

Not everyone is celebrating. The shift to agentic search raises serious concerns:

  • Publishers and content creators fear that AI-generated answers will further reduce traffic to their websites — the same concern driving the DuckDuckGo surge.
  • Privacy advocates worry that information agents require deep personalization, meaning Google will know even more about users' interests, habits, and intentions.
  • Small businesses are uncertain whether their websites will be discoverable in an AI-first search environment, or whether Google's AI will simply answer customer questions without directing traffic to business sites.

What This Means for You

If you're a Google user, the agentic search features will roll out gradually throughout 2026. The basic experience will be free, but advanced features like Information Agents require a Google AI subscription. Whether you embrace or resist these changes, one thing is clear: the way we find information online is fundamentally changing, and there's no going back.
