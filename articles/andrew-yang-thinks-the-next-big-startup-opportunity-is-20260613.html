Andrew Yang thinks the next big startup opportunity is lowering the cost of living

✍️ Hussein 📅 June 13, 2026 ⏱️ 4 min read 📰 TechCrunch
📰 Via TechCrunch

Ever feel like your wallet has a permanent leak, constantly draining cash for the absolute necessities? You’re not alone. For millions of Americans, the cost of simply existing has become an overwhelming burden, a relentless uphill battle against rising prices for everything from the roof over their heads to the food on their plates. It’s a reality that hits close to home for many, myself included, and it’s precisely this pervasive problem that Andrew Yang, the entrepreneur and former presidential candidate, believes holds the key to the next massive startup opportunity.

Yang, a name synonymous with forward-thinking ideas and often challenging the status quo, recently dropped a bombshell observation via TechCrunch: Americans are systematically overpaying for a whole host of essential goods and services. He’s talking about the big ones – housing, food, wireless internet – the foundational pillars of modern life that have become shockingly expensive. His audacious, yet incredibly logical, thesis is that the next wave of entrepreneurial billionaires won't be made by creating entirely new gadgets or social networks, but by giving that money back to people. By finding ways to drastically lower the cost of living, startups could tap into an economic goldmine, while simultaneously solving one of society’s most pressing issues.

Think about it: instead of chasing after the next fleeting trend, what if the focus shifted to fundamental human needs? This isn't just about charity; it's about smart business. When you can deliver essential services at a significantly lower cost, you unlock immense market potential. It’s a concept that resonates deeply with the mission here at AI Profit Hub, as we consistently explore how cutting-edge technology, particularly artificial intelligence, can drive both innovation and tangible value for people.

The Overlooked Goldmine: Stripping Away Excess Costs

Yang’s insight isn't just a casual observation; it's a stark indictment of market inefficiencies and entrenched systems. He pinpointed areas where, frankly, we're being fleeced. Housing, for example, is a colossal beast. From construction costs to real estate agent fees, mortgage interest, and property taxes, the layers of expense are staggering. Then there’s food: supply chain complexities, massive retail markups, and food waste all contribute to grocery bills that feel like a second rent payment.

Wireless services? Another prime example. Despite incredible technological advancements, many consumers are still locked into expensive plans with limited flexibility, paying for bandwidth they don't always use, all while providers rake in billions. These aren't just minor irritations; they represent significant drains on household budgets, collectively amounting to trillions of dollars annually. The sheer scale of this "overpayment" means that even small percentage reductions translate into massive savings for consumers and, consequently, enormous market share for the disruptors.

But how exactly do you "give that money back"? This is where the magic of innovation, powered by AI, truly shines. AI isn't just about automation; it's about optimization, prediction, personalization, and creating hyper-efficiency that was previously unimaginable. Imagine AI-driven construction techniques that reduce building costs by 30%, or smart logistics networks that cut food waste and delivery expenses by half. Think about AI algorithms that dynamically optimize your wireless plan based on real-time usage, saving you hundreds of dollars a year. These aren't futuristic fantasies; they are capabilities that AI is already starting to deliver. The opportunity is to apply them systemically to these high-cost sectors.

Industry Implications and AI-Powered Opportunities

The implications of Yang's vision, especially when viewed through an AI lens, are nothing short of revolutionary. Every industry currently benefiting from these "overpayments" is ripe for disruption. This isn't just about incremental improvements; it's about a fundamental restructuring of how these services are delivered and consumed. Here are some key areas primed for an AI-powered shake-up:

  • Housing & Real Estate:
    • AI-Optimized Construction: From generative design to robotic construction and predictive maintenance, AI can dramatically lower building costs and operational expenses for homes and apartments. Think modular homes designed by AI for maximum efficiency, or AI-powered smart grids that slash utility bills.
    • Decentralized Real Estate Platforms: AI-powered platforms can match buyers and sellers more efficiently, reduce brokerage fees, and even automate legal processes, making homeownership more accessible and affordable.
    • Smart Home Energy Management: AI systems that learn usage patterns and optimize energy consumption, leading to significant savings on electricity and heating/cooling.
  • Food & Groceries:
    • Hyper-Efficient Supply Chains: AI can predict demand with unprecedented accuracy, optimize logistics routes, minimize waste from farm to fork, and even manage inventory in real-time for retailers, passing savings directly to consumers.
    • Personalized Nutrition & Meal Planning: AI apps that create hyper-customized meal plans based on budget, dietary needs, and local store deals, helping families eat healthier for less.
    • Local Vertical Farms & Robotics: AI-managed indoor farms can produce fresh, local produce year-round with minimal land and water, drastically cutting transportation costs and spoilage.
