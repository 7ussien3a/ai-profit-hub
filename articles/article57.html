DeepSeek raises $7.5 billion in a historic financing round: Are we close to AGI?

✍ Hussein 📅 Last Updated: Jul 12, 2026 ⏱ 10 min read 📰 Pandaily
📰 Via Pandaily

A revolution in the world of Chinese artificial intelligence and a game-changer

In a move that sent successive shock waves through the global AI industry, leading Chinese AI startup DeepSeek announced the completion of its first-ever external financing round in early July 2026, raising an astonishing and historic amount of US$7.5 billion (roughly equivalent to 51 billion Chinese yuan).

This massive financing round not only makes DeepSeek the most funded private AI company in Chinese history, but it also indicates a profound and pivotal strategic shift in the company's journey. After the company was quietly operating as an independent, self-sufficient research laboratory under the banner of its genius founder, Liang Wenfeng, who rejected commercialization and involvement in funding wars, the company has now turned into a huge and aggressive commercial enterprise that clearly aims to achieve artificial general intelligence (AGI).

The company's financial valuation now stands at approximately US$59 billion, making it one of the most valuable private technology companies in the world. This is a number that seemed like a fantasy just 18 months ago when DeepSeek was just an internal research arm of a quantitative trading company unknown to the public. Today, the company competes side by side and directly with Western technology giants such as OpenAI and Anthropic, and the Google DeepMind division in the most difficult global standards for artificial intelligence, achieving results that place it among the best in the world.

Details of the historic financing round: amazing numbers and facts

The extraordinary financing round is structured as a limited partnership managed by Liang Wenfeng himself, with participating investors subject to a mandatory five-year lock-up period. This means that investors cannot withdraw their money or sell their shares during this period.

This unusual and financially tough structure ensures that Deep Sec's founder maintains absolute operational control and strategic decision-making. This was a non-negotiable condition, and although it initially made some institutional investors hesitant and withdraw from the negotiations, it ultimately discouraged major strategic backers who saw enormous long-term value and unlimited potential in the technology being developed.

The size of this funding is unprecedented by all standards in the history of Chinese artificial intelligence and is equivalent to the budgets of entire countries. To illustrate and make a simple comparison, the entire AI department at Alibaba Group received about $13 billion in strategic capital over three full years; While Deep Sec secured more than half of this huge amount in just one round spanning a few weeks.

Deepsec's sudden, rapid rise reflects not only its astonishing technical achievements and algorithmic superiority, but also a complete shift in the Chinese government's strategic priorities. Artificial intelligence and large language models now rank alongside advanced semiconductors and clean energy as a critical national security asset and a key determinant of technological superpower in the 21st century.

Who are the investors and why are they investing billions now?

This financing round saw the entry of prominent names in the world of technology and industry in China, each seeking to achieve its own strategic goals through this huge investment:

Tencent: The social media and gaming giant in China, which has the largest digital revenues, provides not only capital and liquidity, but also a huge and interconnected ecosystem that includes more than 1.3 billion active users of the WeChat application, in addition to a massive cloud infrastructure dedicated to institutional businesses. Tencent's investment is widely interpreted in tech business circles as a strategic hedge against its internal AI teams falling behind rapidly, while ensuring early and direct access to what many consider to be China's best and fastest pioneering model. We'll likely see DeepSeek models integrated into Tencent's search engines and games soon.

CATL Company: The world's largest manufacturer of electric car batteries and a primary supplier to companies like Tesla and others, it is perhaps the most surprising backer in this round. Industry analysts and experts interpret CATL's significant investment as part of a broader, longer-term strategy to integrate advanced AI directly into battery management and grid optimization systems. The company also seeks to leverage Deep Sec's complex thinking capabilities to accelerate materials science research and development of new chemical compounds for solid-state batteries.

China's National Artificial Intelligence Industry Investment Fund: This massive fund provides the government's stamp of approval and official political umbrella. The fund, directly supported and directed by the State Council, has historically invested in technology companies of the highest strategic importance. Their presence on this tour clearly confirms that DeepSec has moved from being a startup to being considered a critical national infrastructure for China.

What is particularly striking and impressive in investment circles is the contribution of the founder himself, as Liang Wenfeng personally injected nearly $2.9 billion of his own wealth into the round. This behavior reflects his absolute deep personal conviction in the project's success, maintains his dominant ownership position, and sends a strong reassuring message to other investors that the captain of the ship is putting his own money on the line alongside them.

The radical shift towards artificial general intelligence (AGI)

For two full years, DeepSec operated under a deliberately non-commercial philosophy, focusing entirely on efficient coding and advanced mathematics. Liang Wenfeng has repeatedly stated on multiple occasions that the company will never seek outside financing, will never go public on the stock exchange, nor will it aggressively market its models or chase immediate revenue. The ultimate and only goal, he said then, was pure, pure research, understanding the mysteries of human intelligence at a fundamental level, and finding algorithms that mimic human thinking without the pressures of quarterly revenues and pesky investor expectations.

Today, this philosophy has been explicitly and categorically abandoned. In press releases and internal communications surrounding the funding round, DeepSeek stated clearly and in an unwavering tone: “Humanity is literally on the cusp of artificial general intelligence (AGI).”

The company's management argues that the computing infrastructure needed to achieve general artificial intelligence can in no way be financed internally or through self-financing, regardless of the revenues. Rather, it requires the kind of capital that is usually allocated to giant infrastructure projects at the national level (such as dams, high-speed train networks, or space programs).

The massive hiring drive that followed the funding announcement is astonishing in its ambition and scope: DeepSec plans to at least double the size of every department within the company over the next six months. Recruitment is heavily focused on attracting the best minds in algorithmic research, parallel systems engineering, and performance optimization experts. This rapid transition and transformation from an agile, small-scale research laboratory to a massive commercial engineering organization closely mirrors and matches the historical path followed by the American company OpenAI between 2019 and 2022, a similarity that is keenly noted by all observers in Silicon Valley and Beijing alike.

The sovereign bet on Huawei Ascend chips

The explicit and public focus on relying on Huawei Ascend 950PR chips rather than hardware and GPUs from the American company NVIDIA is one of the most important strategic and geopolitical aspects of Deep Sec's capital deployment plan. Strict and increasingly stringent US export restrictions have gradually and painfully limited China's access to NVIDIA's most advanced GPUs, creating a real crisis, but at the same time creating a huge opportunity to accelerate the pace of domestic chip development and push Chinese companies into interdependence.

DeepSec's previous V3 model has been significantly improved and an exceptional software effort has been made to run incredibly efficiently on Huawei's Ascend 910 series, exceeding many Western expectations. The upcoming V4 model, expected to appear in mid-July 2026, is being designed and trained from scratch specifically to be compatible with the latest Ascend 950PR chip architecture that has not yet been released to the public.

This deep relationship in co-development between two giant Chinese entities creates a highly productive and virtuous cycle: DeepSEC's improvements at the software and algorithm level make Huawei's chips more efficient, commercially viable and globally competitive, while Huawei's hardware improvements and doubling of memory and transfer speed open up new and advanced DeepSEC modeling capabilities that are not otherwise achievable.

The strategic implications of this alliance extend far beyond the two companies. Based on this transformation, Huawei's AI chip revenues are expected to grow by more than 60% in 2026 alone, reaching approximately US$12 billion. Which simply means that China is succeeding in building a supply chain and an entire artificial intelligence ecosystem that operates completely independently and outside the framework of American technology controls.

Deep Sec V4 Model: What to expect in July 2026?

The highly anticipated V4 is shaping up to be Deep Sec's most ambitious, most expensive, and most impactful release to date. While full technical specifications and parameter numbers are still being kept strictly under wraps, several industry sources and tech leaks indicate that the model will include significantly improved multi-modal capabilities, allowing it to understand, analyze and generate complex video, 3D audio and high-resolution images seamlessly.

Besides, the model is expected to feature amazing improvement in logic and reasoning in very long contexts, an innovative and all-new architecture specially designed for efficient inference and high parallelism on domestic Chinese chips to circumvent the memory bottleneck.

According to reports from channel partners, DeepSec is also implementing new, flexible API pricing strategies designed to make the advanced model commercially available at affordable prices for startups and SMEs. It's a direct and aggressive competitive move against OpenAI's GPT-5.6 models and recent Claude models. Historically, the company has conquered markets because it has priced its services 5 to 10 times lower than comparable US models, a strategy that appears set to continue as a method for penetrating the global market.

Global Impact: Reshaping the Tech Balance of Power

DeepSec's $7.5 billion funding round represents more than just a financial milestone for one passing company. It is a clear signal and solemn declaration of China's determination as a country and system to compete aggressively and relentlessly on the AI ​​front regardless of Western technology limitations and hardware embargoes.

For American AI companies and policymakers in Washington, the implications are worth pondering and worrying about. OpenAI has raised about $40 billion so far from Microsoft and others; Anthropic received more than $12 billion from Amazon and Google. Despite the huge disparity in funding and unlimited access to NVIDIA chips, DeepSEC operates with a fraction of these resources and cheaper human capital, but it has consistently produced models that compete strongly and break records. With $7.5 billion in new capital and Huawei silicon firmly establishing itself, the technology performance gap could narrow and disappear completely within the next 18 months.

The artificial intelligence race is no longer just a bilateral competition confined to the OpenAI laboratories and Google headquarters in California. The emergence of Deep Sec as a real and fierce competitor for the throne of artificial general intelligence clearly indicates that the period 2026-2028 may witness a truly global three-way competition. China, the United States, and Europe are following very different geopolitical and technological paths toward the ultimate goal: artificial general intelligence that will reshape the global economy.

💬 HUSSEIN'S TAKE

Contrary to what is being marketed in the hasty technical media, this huge funding round does not necessarily mean an immediate and inevitable superiority in the quality of upcoming models at the expense of OpenAI. The painful truth that many do not tell is that Deep Sec was forced to receive this massive funding mainly to compensate for the existing gap in the efficiency of the hardware; The Chinese Ascend chip, no matter how powerful and advanced it is, still consumes energy and effort and requires larger servers to achieve the same results and train the same models achieved by advanced Nvidia chips. This huge investment is a heavy tax on “technical independence” and not just on pure R&D. The real and decisive indicator of the success of this historic step will not be in the launch of the V4 model itself to top the evaluation tables for a few days, but rather in the company’s superior ability to achieve positive and sustainable operating profit margins when running API interfaces on a scale of two billion on local Chinese hardware in light of the fierce competition in pricing.
