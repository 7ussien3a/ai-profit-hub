BIS Warning: Will the AI bubble burst soon?

✍ Hussein 📅 Last Updated: Jul 12, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read 📰 Official Source
📰 Via Official Source

In light of the insane rise in the shares of technology and artificial intelligence companies over the past few years, a fundamental and fateful question arises in the minds of investors and financial analysts: Are we facing a sustainable technical revolution that will change the shape of the global economy forever, or are we just living in a new economic bubble about to burst? In the midst of this excessive optimism, he issued Bank for International Settlements (BIS), which is known as the “bank of central banks,” issued a clear and direct warning to investors and global markets about the risks of a dangerous bubble forming in the artificial intelligence sector.

This warning did not come out of nowhere, but was based on careful monitoring of capital flows and astronomical valuations that are often disproportionate to actual revenues. In this comprehensive article, we will review in detail the reasons for this warning, how it could affect the future of investment in artificial intelligence, and what investors should do to avoid the repercussions of the collapse of the artificial intelligence market if this expected bubble bursts.

What is the BIS's warning about artificial intelligence?

The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) warning mainly focuses on the fact that current valuations of AI companies are seriously overstated and do not reflect the true financial returns at the moment. The bank indicates in its reports that the flow of huge investments into the technical sector is very similar to the period of the “dot-com bubble” in the late 1990s, which portends the possibility of a sharp correction in the financial markets if these companies are unable to achieve sustainable profits that justify these astronomical valuations.

Reasons why the Bank for International Settlements is concerned about the artificial intelligence stock bubble

The BIS warning did not come out of nowhere, but was based on a clear set of economic and financial indicators that cause serious concern. The most prominent reasons that were highlighted are:

1. Unjustified rise in company valuations Global stock markets, especially indices like the Nasdaq, are seeing a heavy focus on a very small number of big technology companies (such as Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Meta) that are leading the AI wave. This unprecedented concentration makes entire markets fragile and vulnerable to sharp fluctuations if these companies' profits decline for any reason or they face unexpected regulatory challenges. Current valuations assume continuous and unhindered growth for decades to come, an assumption that is very difficult to achieve in a world of technology full of fierce competition and rapid developments.

2. The deep gap between technical innovation and achieving profits Despite the amazing development and quantum leaps in large linguistic models (LLMs) and generative AI technologies, many companies, both startups and large companies, have not yet discovered sustainable business models capable of generating tangible profits that cover the enormous development costs. Investments are being made in the tens of billions in developing hard infrastructure and training complex models, but the return on investment (ROI) is still unclear or long-term for many commercial projects.

3. Over-reliance on future promises and marketing Many current investments are built on rosy promises that artificial intelligence will change everything in the near future and eliminate many economic problems. This excessive optimism and aggressive marketing by technology leaders leads individual and institutional investors to ignore potential and obvious risks, such as increasing regulatory restrictions, complex challenges related to copyright and privacy, and physical limitations such as the massive power consumption of data centers and unprecedented demand for silicon chips.

Comparison with the dot-com crisis: Will history repeat itself?

In its analysis, the Bank for International Settlements points to worrying similarities between the current situation and the “dot-com” phase of the late 1990s and the beginning of the new millennium. At that time, any company with an “e” or a “com” in its name had ridiculous stock market valuations without a clear plan for making profits. Today, we see a similar pattern where it is enough for any company to include the word “AI” or “artificial intelligence” in its business plan to see an unjustified jump in its stock prices. This herd investment behavior is the hallmark of all financial bubbles throughout history.

How could an AI market collapse happen?

If the worst-case scenario comes true and the bubble bursts, the collapse of the artificial intelligence market will not be gradual and quiet, but rather it may be sharp and sudden, with devastating repercussions. This usually happens when investors realize, perhaps based on disappointing quarterly earnings reports, that expected profits from AI projects will not materialize in the quick time frame they had bet on.

A massive sell-off in technology company stocks will then begin, which will lead to a sharp decline in their market value in a few days or weeks. This collapse will not only affect specialized technology companies, but will spread as a rapid contagion to mutual funds, pension funds, and the broader economy as a whole, given the heavy and influential weight that these giant companies represent in global financial indicators today.

The future of investing in artificial intelligence after the warning

Despite these serious warnings and well-founded fears, no rational analyst can deny that AI is indeed a revolutionary technology that will continue to grow and shape the future of humanity in the coming decades. Therefore, the future of investment in artificial intelligence must move from the “hype” stage to the “practical adoption & maturity” stage.

Smart investors looking for sustainability in their portfolios will start looking for companies that are truly using AI to solve complex problems, achieve tangible operational efficiencies and reduce costs, rather than companies that merely sell user interface (UI) promises built on open source models. This will include a focus on sectors such as healthcare (drug discovery), logistics and supply chains, and rigorous financial risk analysis.

Roles of regulators in reining in the bubble

The Bank for International Settlements plays a pivotal and essential role in alerting central banks and government regulators around the world to take preventive measures before disaster strikes. It is highly anticipated that we will see a significant increase in regulatory scrutiny of AI companies. This scrutiny will not only be from an antitrust or privacy protection perspective, but also and above all from a “financial stability” perspective.

Future legislation may require publicly traded companies to provide greater and more precise transparency about how they use AI technologies, and to clarify the proportion of actual revenues directly related to these technologies. This level of transparency will greatly help in providing a clearer and rational picture to investors, and reduce the amount of random speculation fueled by rumors and excessive expectations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the stern warning issued by the Bank for International Settlements is a timely and powerful reminder that financial markets are unforgiving of excessive optimism that is not supported by solid economic fundamentals. We may see a harsh correction soon in the valuations of the AI ​​stock bubble, but the established fact is that the technology itself is here to stay and continue to evolve. Investors around the world are required today to be extremely cautious, carefully re-evaluate their investment portfolios to avoid the repercussions of any potential collapse, and focus instead on the real long-term value of companies that are building the foundations of tomorrow.

Frequently asked questions

1. What does the term “AI bubble” mean? The artificial intelligence bubble means an exceptional and unjustified rise in the share prices of companies related to artificial intelligence based on excessive optimism and huge future promises, without this price rise being matched by real profits or revenues that support or justify these very high valuations compared to historical averages.

2. Will the bursting of this bubble lead to the disappearance of artificial intelligence technologies? Of course not, bursting the bubble will not erase technology from existence. Rather, it will lead to a necessary financial and economic adjustment process that eliminates weak and opportunistic companies, while strong companies capable of creating real value will continue to grow and develop innovations at a more rational and sustainable pace, just as happened with the Internet after the 2000 crisis.

3. How can I protect my investments from a possible AI market collapse? To protect your investments in light of this uncertainty, it is highly recommended to diversify your investment portfolio and not put all your money in one sector. Avoid blindly getting carried away by the hype (FOMO), and focus on investing in companies that have clear business models, solid economic moats, and are already generating tangible profits from the use of AI technologies.

💬 HUSSEIN'S TAKE

:An analytical point of view Contrary to what is marketed in the technical media, the BIS’s warning does not mean the end of the artificial intelligence revolution at all, but rather it is a healthy and necessary alarm bell for the return of rationality to the markets. What we see today is a literal repetition of a historical pattern that accompanies every revolutionary new technological innovation; Exaggerated investment hype followed by a painful correction, then a phase of slow, sustained growth. The real challenge is not the technology itself, as it has proven its worth. Rather, the problem lies in the unrealistic expectations of investors who are looking for quick astronomical profits in immature markets. The next correction, whether it happens tomorrow or next year, will be an inevitable “liquidation” of the market, as shell companies with no real value or economic moats to protect them will disappear, and companies that build original artificial intelligence applications that solve tangible economic problems with great efficiency will emerge. Investment now must be very selective and cautious, and based on tangible returns, not future promises. The winners are those with unique data and infrastructure, not just intelligent interfaces.
