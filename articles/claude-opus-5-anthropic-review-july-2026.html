Claude Opus 5: Anthropic's Most Practical Model Yet — Full Review

✍ Hussein 📅 Last Updated: Jul 28, 2026 ⏱ 7 min read 📰 https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5
📰 Via https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24, 2026, and it is arguably the most strategically important model the company has launched all year. Not because it is the most powerful — that distinction still belongs to Claude Fable 5 — but because it delivers performance that approaches Fable 5 at exactly half the price. For developers, enterprises, and power users who found Fable 5 economically impractical for high-volume tasks, Opus 5 is the model they have been waiting for.

At $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens, Claude Opus 5 sits at the same price point as its predecessor, Opus 4.8, but with substantially improved capabilities. It is immediately available via the Claude API, Claude.ai (as the top model on Claude Pro), and was integrated into GitHub Copilot on launch day — which signals just how seriously Anthropic is targeting the developer and enterprise market.

What Is Claude Opus 5? The Essentials in 60 Words

What makes Claude Opus 5 different from previous Claude models? Claude Opus 5 is Anthropic's latest high-performance model that delivers near-flagship performance at flagship-minus-50% cost. It features a 1-million-token context window, 128,000 max output tokens, extended "thinking" enabled by default, and native GitHub Copilot integration. It is positioned specifically for complex coding tasks and deep knowledge work.

Key Specifications and Capabilities

Before diving into the analysis, here are the hard facts you need to know:

Context Window: 1 million tokens. This is enormous — roughly 750,000 words, or the entire content of approximately 7-10 full-length books processed in a single conversation. For enterprise use cases involving large codebases, lengthy legal documents, or complex research archives, this is a genuine game-changer.

Max Output Tokens: 128,000. This allows for extremely long-form outputs — comprehensive code files, full research reports, and extended technical documentation — without requiring session breaks.

Thinking Mode: Enabled by default. Unlike models where extended reasoning is an optional feature, Opus 5 defaults to showing its reasoning process. This is a significant architectural choice: Anthropic is betting that visible, structured reasoning produces more reliable outputs for complex tasks. Early testing supports this.

Pricing: $5 input / $25 output per million tokens. Identical to Opus 4.8, making the upgrade essentially free for existing users.

New Features: Mid-conversation tool changes (currently in beta) — a feature that allows agents to dynamically update their toolset during a task — and improved automatic safety fallbacks.

Performance: How Does It Compare to Claude Fable 5?

Anthropic describes Opus 5 as delivering performance that is "approaching" Fable 5, rather than matching it. Based on early API testing and community reports, this is an accurate characterization. On coding benchmarks like SWE-bench, Opus 5 scores in the high-tier range — meaningfully above Claude Sonnet 5 but slightly below Fable 5's ceiling.

Where Opus 5 genuinely matches or exceeds Fable 5 in practical terms is in agentic and multi-step tasks. The combination of the 1M context window, default thinking, and mid-conversation tool updates makes Opus 5 the stronger choice for automated workflows that require the model to maintain context across extended operations. Fable 5 remains superior for tasks requiring the absolute highest single-inference accuracy — think complex scientific reasoning or intricate mathematical proofs.

For the vast majority of enterprise use cases — code review, document analysis, customer service automation, research synthesis — Opus 5 will be indistinguishable from Fable 5 in output quality while costing significantly less at scale.

GitHub Copilot Integration: The Strategic Play

The same-day GitHub Copilot integration is not a coincidence. It is Anthropic's most direct assault yet on Microsoft and OpenAI's dominant position in the developer tools market.

GitHub Copilot has over 1.8 million paid subscribers and is deeply embedded in the workflows of professional developers worldwide. By launching Opus 5 as a Copilot-integrated model on day one, Anthropic bypasses the usual adoption curve — developers don't need to switch tools, they just need to select Opus 5 as their preferred model within an environment they already use daily.

The practical implication: any developer who found Claude Sonnet 5 slightly underpowered for complex architectural tasks but found Fable 5 too expensive for everyday coding assistance now has an obvious answer. Opus 5 at Copilot scale pricing is positioned to capture this middle segment of the professional developer market.

AMD Partnership: $5 Billion for Infrastructure at Scale

Claude Opus 5's launch coincides with a major infrastructure announcement: on July 22, AMD and Anthropic announced a $5 billion strategic partnership involving the deployment of AMD's Instinct MI450 series GPUs to scale Anthropic's inference capacity.

This is significant for several reasons. First, it reduces Anthropic's dependence on NVIDIA's H100/H200/B200 series — a geopolitical and supply-chain resilience move as much as a financial one. Second, it signals that Anthropic is preparing for a substantial increase in inference demand — suggesting the company expects Opus 5 adoption to significantly exceed that of its predecessors. Third, AMD's involvement gives the semiconductor giant a flagship customer reference for its AI inference hardware, creating a mutually reinforcing marketing dynamic.

Comparison: Claude Opus 5 vs. the Competition

The table reveals Opus 5's clearest positioning: it offers the best combination of capability and cost for complex, context-heavy tasks when compared to Western closed models. Its primary competitive threat is not from Fable 5 or GPT-5.6, but from the emerging Chinese models — particularly Qwen 3.8-Max — which may undercut it significantly on price if Alibaba delivers on its open-weight promise.

Conclusion

Claude Opus 5 is the most practical Claude model Anthropic has ever shipped. At $5/$25 per million tokens, with a 1M context window and default thinking enabled, it removes the primary barrier that prevented high-volume enterprise adoption of Anthropic's top-tier models: cost. For developers building agentic workflows, enterprises running large-scale document processing, and researchers handling complex analytical tasks, Opus 5 is the immediate upgrade path. Whether it can hold its market position as Chinese open-source models mature is the more interesting long-term question.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How does Claude Opus 5 compare to Claude Fable 5? Opus 5 delivers performance approaching Fable 5 (particularly for coding and agentic tasks) at roughly half the price. Fable 5 retains an edge in tasks requiring absolute maximum reasoning accuracy, such as advanced scientific or mathematical work.

Is Claude Opus 5 available on GitHub Copilot? Yes — Anthropic integrated Opus 5 into GitHub Copilot on the same day as the API launch, July 24, 2026. Copilot subscribers can select it as their preferred model.

What is the context window of Claude Opus 5? Claude Opus 5 features a 1-million-token context window, equivalent to approximately 750,000 words — enough to process entire large codebases or multiple book-length documents in a single conversation.

💬 HUSSEIN'S TAKE

Claude Opus 5 is a smart product move, but read between the lines. Anthropic is under pressure. The revenue gap with OpenAI is narrowing but still real, and the open-source wave — Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8-Max preview, and whatever DeepSeek releases next — is eroding the economic case for expensive proprietary models. Opus 5 is Anthropic's answer to that pressure: keep the Fable 5 brand for prestige benchmarks, but give the market something practical and affordable that retains the "Anthropic quality" halo. The GitHub Copilot integration is particularly telling — Anthropic knows it cannot win on model quality alone in a world where the capability gap is closing. It needs distribution, and Microsoft's 1.8 million Copilot subscribers are an extremely attractive shortcut. Opus 5 is a good model. But it is also a survival strategy.
